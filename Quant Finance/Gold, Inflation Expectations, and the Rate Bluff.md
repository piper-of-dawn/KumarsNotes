# Gold is not impressed by your rate-cut story

Gold has a simple public myth.

Rates down. Gold up.

Nice story. Clean. Easy to repeat on a podcast while pretending a futures curve is a personality trait.

The data is less polite.

Gold does not move because one macro variable clears its throat. Gold moves when the market starts repricing the paper around it. Inflation expectations matter. Real yields matter. The dollar matters. Stress matters. Central-bank buying matters. Positioning matters.

The lazy version says gold is a rate-cut trade.

The better version says gold is a distrust trade. It likes moments when the money looks weaker, the policy story looks tired, and the market starts marking up inflation risk.

## The fixed-price era is a trap

The gold series starts in 1833. That sounds impressive until you look at the early data.

For long stretches, the price barely moves. Not because gold was calm. Because the monetary system put it in a cage and called the cage stability.

If you fit a trend through that whole history, you are mixing two different animals. Fixed-price gold and market-price gold. That is not analysis. That is pouring coffee into a fuel tank and asking why the engine sounds philosophical.

So the trend starts in April 1968.

That is the cleaner cut. Not perfect. Cleaner.

## The regression

The dependent variable is monthly gold log return.

Not price level.

Price levels trend. Trended variables flatter you. They hand you fake confidence with a straight face. Returns are harsher. Good. You want harsh.

The model is simple:

```text
gold_log_return ~ fed_funds_change + breakeven_change
```

Inputs:

- Fed funds expectations: 30-day fed funds futures, `ZQ=F`, converted into an implied rate as `100 - futures_price`.
- Inflation expectations: 10-year breakeven inflation, `T10YIEM`.
- Sample: 2003-02-01 to 2026-05-01.
- Observations: 280 monthly periods.

Breakevens are not magic. They are messy. They include inflation expectations, inflation risk premia, liquidity, and market plumbing. Fine.

Markets are messy. Your job is not to find purity. Your job is to find useful dirt.

## The trade

If you are watching gold, stop treating every Fed whisper like scripture.

Watch what the market says about inflation compensation.

Watch breakevens.

Watch real yields.

Watch the dollar.

Watch whether gold is rising because inflation fear is being repriced, or because everyone is hiding under the same shiny blanket after the fact.

The regression R-squared is 0.043. That is low. Good. It keeps us honest.

This model explains a small slice of monthly gold returns. The rest lives in flows, geopolitics, central-bank buying, dollar pressure, crisis demand, and the usual market theatre where everyone pretends their favorite chart is a law of physics.

## The warning

The rate-cut story is too clean.

Clean stories sell. Dirty mechanisms pay.

Gold does not care about your preferred narrative. It responds to pressure in the system. Inflation pressure. Credibility pressure. Currency pressure. Policy pressure.

When breakevens move, gold listens.

When fed funds expectations move, gold shrugs more often than the macro tourists admit.

That is the tradeoff. That is the signal. Everything else is decoration with a Bloomberg terminal.
