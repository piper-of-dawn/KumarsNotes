1. The forward price is set at initiation such that the value of the contract is zero to both parties (otherwise there would be arbitrage).

> [!tip] HAMMER THIS INTO YOUR HEAD
> Price of a forward is cost of buying a stock now plus cost of carrying it to maturity. Internalising this sentence will take you far.
> 
> PRICE OF FORWARD CONTRACT = SPOT + COST OF CARRY - BENEFITS - PV OF FORWARD PRICE
> 
> $$ V_T = S_0 + PV(C) - F_T (1+R_f)^{-T} - PV(B)$$
> 
> No arbitrage condition implies $V_T = 0$
> 
> So $F_T (1+R_f)^{-T} = S_0 + PV(C) - PV(B)$
> 
> **Higher $R_f$ means higher cost of carry. Think, I sell the underlying today and invest the proceeds @ $R_f$, by holding the underlying I am sacrificing $R_f$**
> 
> High $R_f$ means heavily discounted forward payment and hence high price of contract today

2. **Backwardation**: future/forward < spot. Asset has large benefits that has to be compensated with lower forward price. 
3. **Contango:** future/forward > spot. Asset has high cost of carry, which needs to be compensated with higher forward price. Remember: ==Price of a forward is cost of buying a stock now plus cost of carrying it to maturity.==
