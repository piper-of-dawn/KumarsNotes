> [!abstract] MEMORISE
> - Macaulay duration = weighted-average time to cash flows.
> - It is a time measure, not directly a price-change measure.
> - For a zero-coupon bond, Macaulay duration equals maturity.
> - As a bond moves closer to maturity, duration usually falls.

1. Macaulay duration is the **present-value-weighted average time** to receive the bond’s cash flows. It is still a time concept, not directly a price-change concept.
2. For a zero-coupon bond, Macaulay duration equals maturity because there is only one cash flow: principal at the end.
3. Duration is not static. As a bond moves closer to maturity, duration falls. Time itself slowly bleeds interest-rate risk out of the bond.
