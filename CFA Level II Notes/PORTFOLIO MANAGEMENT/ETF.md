## SEE THIS BEFORE EXAM

### Which ETFs are preferred for tactical trading?

Answer: ETFs with high trading volume and sufficient liquidity within the desired asset class.

Common mistake: Choosing the ETF with the lowest management fee. For short-term trading, transaction costs matter more than small differences in annual fees.

### 2. Which product is least correctly associated with settlement risk?

Answer: ETNs. Their primary risk is issuer credit risk, because they're unsecured debt obligations.

Common mistake: Confusing ETNs with synthetic ETFs. Synthetic ETFs use swaps and face counterparty and settlement risks.

### 3. Why might an ETF trade at a premium or discount to NAV?

Answer: Different market closing times can make NAV stale, particularly for international ETFs.

Common mistake: Blaming representative sampling. Sampling causes tracking error, not directly a premium or discount to NAV.

### 4. Which ETF is best for a tactical investment in a specific technology segment?

Answer: ETF 3 — a thematic ETF with relatively high trading volume (7.5× average assets), despite its higher 20 bps management fee.

Common mistake: Choosing ETF 2 because it has the highest liquidity and lowest fee, while ignoring that its broad-market exposure doesn't match the intended investment.

### 5. Which factor is NOT a source of ETF tracking error?

Answer: The frequency of index tracking, according to the CFA question.

Common mistake: Selecting representative sampling. Holding only a subset of index securities can cause ETF returns to deviate from benchmark returns.

Exam rule: First identify what the investor wants to achieve. Then distinguish exposure, liquidity, pricing differences, and tracking error.

3. Highly liquid ETFs may trade actively all day, and **hence they do not rely on the creation and redemption process behind the scenes.**
4. Authorized participants work with the ETF issuer. They buy or sell the underlying basket of securities to create ETF shares or redeem them for destruction.
5. The authorized participant typically absorbs the costs of creating and redeeming ETF shares, then passes them through to the parties making the trade, which ultimately bear them. 
6. Rolling return assessment evaluates how closely an ETF tracks its benchmark over successive, overlapping holding periods. Suppose we use 12-month rolling returns:
    - Calculate the ETF's return from January to December.
    - Calculate the benchmark's return over the same period.
    - Subtract the benchmark return from the ETF return.

7. Although additions and deletions of securities from the underlying benchmark index may occur and result in tracking error, such index changes generally occur infrequently (often quarterly).

A creation basket contains all the underlying securities. For an S&P 500 ETF, that could mean all 500 stocks, weighted by market capitalization.

Creation units are simply the lot size: the number of baskets that must be delivered to create a specified amount of an ETF.

Intraday values and NAVs estimate what an ETF should be worth while the underlying markets are closed.


A redemption basket is the set of underlying securities an authorized participant receives from an ETF issuer when redeeming ETF shares. The issuer publishes it daily.
