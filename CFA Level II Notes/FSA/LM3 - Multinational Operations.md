## Inventory Measurement

5. FIFO
– Ending inventory = newest stuff → more recent rates
– COGS = older stuff → older rates
6. LIFO
– Ending inventory = older stuff → older rates
– COGS = newest stuff → more recent rates
7. Weighted average
– Both ending inventory and COGS get the weighted-average rate for the period. Nice and boring.

Memorise this table:

|Item                             |CR                  |TR                                                                |
|---------------------------------|--------------------|------------------------------------------------------------------|
|Ending Inventory on balance sheet|Always Year End Rate|Weighted Average Rate or Newer Rate if FIFO and Older Rate if FIFO|
|Revenue                          |Weighted Average    |Point in time rate when you sell                                  |



## CTA

8. **==CTA is only measured under current rate method.**== ==Under the temporal method, any remeasurement gain or loss is dumped in the income statement, and there is no plug figure on the balance sheet.== CTA gets recorded in OCI.

Assets = 1,000
Liabilities = 600
Common stock = 150
Beginning RE = 175
Net income = 50
Dividends = 25

Assets  = Equity + Liabilities
        = Common Stock + RE + Liabilities

Ending RE = Beginning RE + Inflow - Outflow
= 175 + 50 - 25 = 200
Common Stock + RE + Liabilities = 150 + 200 + 600 = 950

Assets = 1000

Difference = 50 which is CTA

Balance Sheet Exposures
---
> This is the worst section of the learning module. Take it slowly.

The module gets murkiest around balance sheet exposures. Under the temporal method, the balance can leave you with either a net asset exposure or a net liability exposure.

Balance Sheet Exposure = Exposed Assets - Exposed Liabilities

==Fixed Assets, Intangibles such as goodwills or patents, Deferred Revenue, Prepaid Costs -> All are immune to FX changes under Temporal Method. So they will NOT count into exposed assets.==


Cash, Receivables, Inventory measured at current market value are NOT IMMUNE. So they will be counted as exposed assets. 

Liabilities such as accounts payable, notes payable, deferred tax liabilities, short-term borrowings, long-term debt, bank overdrafts, and accrued expenses are all exposed liabilities.


### Chain of Thought

When you are carrying a net liability exposure, which is usually the case.

To reduce it -> Either increase the assets you have exposed or reduce the exposed liabilities.

- Sell your machine for Cash. Exposed Asset ↑  ↓
- TRAP = Repay your debt using cash. Exposed Asset ↓  and Exposed Liability ↓. SO NO CHANGE in net exposure.

use the reverse analogy when you have net asset exposure.
