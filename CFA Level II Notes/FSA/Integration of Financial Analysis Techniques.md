# Earnings Quality

1. The cash flow-based accruals ratio shows how much of a company's net income lacks support from operating and investing cash flows.

$$\boxed{\text{Cash-flow based accruals quality} = \frac{NI - (CFO + CFI)}{\text{Average NOA}}}$$


NOA = Operating Assets - Operating Liabilities

Operating Assets = Total Assets - Cash
Operating Liabilities = Total Liabilities - Interest Bearing debt

2. One way to assess earnings quality is to divide operating cash flow before interest and taxes by operating income. ==When interest is classified as financing then DO NOT add interest to operating cash flow.==

3. Balance Sheet Based Accruals Ratio. 

NOA = Operating Assets - Operating Liabilities
Operating Assets = Total Assets - Cash

Operating assets can rise sharply when inventory or accounts receivables rise. Paper income is rising, but it is not making the trip into cash. An increase in operating assets would raise net operating assets, so balance sheet-based accruals measure the change in net operating assets relative to average net operating assets. Lower is better.

$$\boxed{\text{Balance Sheet Based Accruals} = \frac{NOA_{t} - NOA_{t-1}}{\text{Avg NOA}}}$$


# Segment Analysis

**Growth Allocation Ratio = % of capital expenditure / % of assets in the segment**

==Decision Rule: If the growth allocation ratio falls below 1, the business wants to cut the segment loose.==

Capital expenditure eventually buys assets. The growth allocation ratio compares a segment's share of capital expenditure with its share of assets, showing where the company is expanding its asset base.

To identify an underperforming segment, we need to calculate the return on capital it generates. One measure is operating income divided by capital employed. Higher is better.
