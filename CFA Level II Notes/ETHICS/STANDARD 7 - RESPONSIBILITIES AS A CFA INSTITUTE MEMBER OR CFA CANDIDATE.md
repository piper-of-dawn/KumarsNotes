## Variant: Discuss the experience, protect the content, describe the credential accurately

**Abstract:** *VII(A) protects program integrity and confidential content; VII(B) controls how membership, candidacy, and the charter are represented. Criticism, factual achievement, and genuine expertise are not automatically violations.*

> A candidate complains about the exam, mentions which formulas were absent, advertises retirement-planning expertise, and describes herself as “CFA Level II.” Which statement fails because of its content, which because of its credential claim, and which may be fine?

The exam does not require enthusiasm for CFA Institute. You can dislike the exam, dislike the process, and say so. It does require you to keep the confidential parts confidential and stop promoting exam progress into a qualification you do not hold.

**VII(A) Conduct as Participants in CFA Institute Programs — opinion is different from disclosure**

> [!NOTE]
> Do not compromise CFA Institute's reputation or integrity, or the integrity, validity, or security of its programs. Actual cheating is one example; prohibited exam conduct and disclosure of confidential content also qualify.

**Case 1.** Two candidates complain publicly about their preparation provider. One says, “The exam was difficult and your material left me underprepared.” The other adds which subjects appeared and which formulas were not tested. Is the second merely expressing a more useful opinion?

> [!NOTE]- Answer and decisive fact
> **No.** The first statement can be a permissible opinion. The second reveals confidential exam coverage; tested and untested material both convey content information.

**Change one fact:** discussing a topic's published curriculum weight is different from reporting its presence or absence in the exam you just sat. The source of the information determines the confidentiality issue.

**Case 2.** A candidate shares remembered questions privately after her session, believing all local candidates have already finished. Another asks an earlier-session candidate what appeared. Does either avoid VII(A) by using a small private group?

> [!NOTE]- Answer and decisive fact
> **No.** Confidentiality does not expire when your session ends. Soliciting confidential exam content is also prohibited; a private audience does not change the information's status.

Do not use “I wasn't sure the leaked questions were authentic” as permission to distribute or solicit apparently confidential material. Ordinary study groups using public curriculum and authorized questions are different.

**Case 3.** An exam volunteer tells clients her role gives them advance access to possible changes in standards. She has not yet sent a confidential draft. Is describing her volunteer role necessarily acceptable because the title is true?

> [!NOTE]- Answer and decisive fact
> **No.** Factually describing service can comply. Presenting privileged access as a client advantage exploits confidential involvement and compromises integrity; a true title does not validate the implied benefit.

**Case 4.** A candidate violates a testing rule but cannot have gained extra marks from it. Another submits false medical documents for a deferral. Are these outside VII(A) because neither copied an answer?

> [!NOTE]- Answer and decisive fact
> **No.** Program integrity includes testing rules and honest dealings with CFA Institute. A successful score advantage is not necessary for a breach; assess the particular conduct and applicable rule.

> [!NOTE]
> You may criticize policies, describe nonconfidential personal experiences, and discuss public study material. You may not disclose or solicit confidential exam questions, coverage, formulas, grading deliberations, or other protected program information.

**VII(B) Reference to CFA Institute, the CFA Designation, and the CFA Program — current status and implied meaning**

> [!NOTE]
> Do not misrepresent or exaggerate membership, candidacy, or the designation. Accurate achievements and educational benefits may be described; implied superior investment ability or performance from the credential may not.

**Case 5.** A firm truthfully says every principal passed the exams on the first attempt. The next sentence explains that this makes its managers better investors than competitors. Is the truthful first sentence sufficient protection?

> [!NOTE]- Answer and decisive fact
> **No.** The fact itself may be stated. Linking it to superior investment ability or performance is the prohibited inference. Read the combined message, not each sentence in isolation.

**Case 6.** One person writes “Passed Level II of the CFA Program” in a résumé. Another puts “CFA Level II” after her name. Both passed the same exam. Why can the answers differ?

> [!NOTE]- Answer and decisive fact
> **The second implies a partial designation.** Passing a level is an achievement that may be stated in words; it does not create a partial CFA credential to attach to a name.

| Representation | What decides it |
|---|---|
| “Passed Level II of the CFA Program” | Permissible if factually true. |
| “Level III candidate in the CFA Program” | Permissible if current candidacy is established. |
| “CFA Level II” / “CFA, Level II” | Improper partial-designation implication. |
| “CFA expected 2027” / “Charter Pending” | Improper expected or pending designation. |
| “The program improved my investment knowledge” | Can describe a genuine educational benefit. |
| “The charter means I deliver superior returns” | Improper performance implication. |

**Case 7.** A candidate passes Level II, receives her result, and takes a study break without enrolling for Level III. May she continue calling herself a Level III candidate because that is her next exam?

> [!NOTE]- Answer and decisive fact
> **No.** In the supplied curriculum, candidacy requires accepted enrollment for a specified examination, or having sat that examination while awaiting results. Intended future enrollment does not establish current candidacy.

She may still describe the level she actually passed. Losing candidate status does not erase the achievement, and the achievement does not preserve candidate status.

**Case 8.** A former charterholder stops paying dues and filing the Professional Conduct Statement, but prints “CFA” on a personal contact card. A retired member maintains the properly approved membership status and required reduced dues. Does retirement itself decide the right to use the designation?

> [!NOTE]- Answer and decisive fact
> **No.** The right depends on meeting applicable membership and reinstatement requirements. A plain personal card is still a representation; properly maintained retired status differs from simply allowing membership to lapse.

> [!NOTE]
> If membership requirements lapse, stop implying current entitlement until reinstated. You may describe historical membership or charterholder status accurately. A pseudonym hiding identity must not be tagged with the CFA designation.

**From your mistakes: Cohen.** Evaluate each online action separately. Improper trademark abbreviations and exam-coverage disclosure can violate different requirements. Claiming retirement-planning expertise is not prohibited merely because he is a candidate; it must still be truthful and supported by competence. The shorter answer “expertise is allowed” does not license invented expertise.

> [!NOTE]
> Websites, biographies, business cards, résumés, oral pitches, and social media carry the same core duties. Informal delivery does not remove a false status claim or an improper performance implication.
