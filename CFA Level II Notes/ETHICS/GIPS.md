## Variant: GIPS compliance, composites, and what verification actually proves

**Abstract:** *Identify the entity claiming compliance, the portfolios belonging in each composite, and the scope of assurance. A fair presentation, a GIPS claim, and independent verification are three different statements.*

> A firm has a strong composite, an independent verifier, and accurate arithmetic. Can it exclude a restricted account, omit a closed account's history, or describe just that composite as GIPS compliant?

GIPS—the **Global Investment Performance Standards**—makes managers' performance presentations more comparable and less vulnerable to selective storytelling. It cannot turn a carefully presented weak strategy into a strong one. The presentation is the map, not the territory.

**Start with the claim**

> [!NOTE]
> Compliance is voluntary to adopt, but a compliance claim requires all applicable requirements. A firm cannot claim partial compliance or compliance for only its favorite composite.

**Case 1.** A manager says, “Our equity composite is GIPS compliant; the firm's other strategies are outside the claim.” Another firm does not claim GIPS, but presents fair, accurate, complete performance under III(D). Which statement creates the problem?

> [!NOTE]- Answer and decisive fact
> **The selective GIPS claim.** Compliance is firm-wide. The second firm can meet III(D) without claiming GIPS compliance; it must still avoid misleading performance presentation.

A software vendor cannot claim its calculator is itself a GIPS-compliant investment firm. Nor does an individual's involvement in a track record make the individual a compliant entity. Define the firm appropriately; the broadest meaningful definition is **recommended**, rather than a license to choose whichever boundary excludes weak results. Relevant applicable laws still govern. [Official overview](https://www.cfainstitute.org/insights/professional-learning/refresher-readings/2026/overview-of-the-global-investment-performance-standards).

**Composites — decide membership before looking at the winners**

A **composite** groups portfolios following a similar investment mandate, objective, or strategy. A **segregated account** is a separately owned client portfolio; a pooled fund holds assets for multiple investors. “Discretionary” here asks whether the manager can implement the intended strategy, rather than merely whether the manager can place orders.

> [!NOTE]
> Include all actual, fee-paying, discretionary segregated accounts in at least one appropriate composite, and matching discretionary pooled funds where required. Non-discretionary portfolios must not enter composites.

**Case 2.** Two accounts prohibit particular stocks. One restriction barely affects implementation; the other makes the advertised strategy impossible. May the manager exclude both merely because each has a restriction?

> [!NOTE]- Answer and decisive fact
> **No.** The restriction's practical effect matters. Document whether the intended strategy can be implemented; do not use the presence of any restriction as a convenient exclusion switch.

**Case 3.** A weak account closes. Can the firm remove its earlier results because it is no longer managed?

> [!NOTE]- Answer and decisive fact
> **No.** Preserve its appropriate historical contribution. Ending the relationship does not erase the period managed; otherwise closed losing accounts disappear and create survivorship bias.

**Case 4.** A firm manages only a pooled fund in a strategy never managed for or offered as a segregated account. Must it create a composite solely for that fund?

> [!NOTE]- Answer and decisive fact
> **Not under the post-2020 pooled-fund-only exception.** Do not apply the segregated-account composite rule mechanically. If a pooled fund meets an applicable composite definition, its inclusion requirements still matter.

These distinctions follow the [official Firms Handbook, Sections 1 and 3](https://www.gipsstandards.org/standards/gips-standards-for-firms/gips-standards-handbook-for-firms/). Membership follows the definition, not how useful the account is to the brochure.

**Verification — what did the independent party examine?**

> [!NOTE]
> Verification is an independent firm-wide assessment of the design and implementation of relevant GIPS policies and procedures. It does not assure the accuracy of a specific performance report, certify skill, or guarantee returns.

**Case 5.** A firm says, “Our Global Equity composite was verified, proving its reported return is correct.” Another says its firm-wide GIPS processes were independently verified for stated periods. Which overclaims the assurance?

> [!NOTE]- Answer and decisive fact
> **The first.** Verification is firm-wide, and its scope differs from a focused performance examination of a particular composite or pooled fund. Do not swap those labels.

**Case 6.** A firm meets the applicable requirements but has not engaged a verifier. Its compliance team has checked the work internally. Can it claim compliance? Can it claim independent verification?

> [!NOTE]- Answer and decisive fact
> **Compliance can be claimed if actually achieved; independent verification cannot.** Verification is recommended, not a condition of compliance, and an internal review is not an independent third-party engagement.

The [official 2026 overview](https://www.cfainstitute.org/insights/professional-learning/refresher-readings/2026/overview-of-the-global-investment-performance-standards) explains verification's voluntary scope. Compliance responsibility remains with the firm; hiring a verifier does not transfer it.

**Asset owners, recipients, and benefits**

> [!NOTE]
> An asset owner reporting its own assets to its oversight body generally uses the Asset Owner standards. When competing for external business, the Firms standards apply to that activity; mixed organizations require careful definition.

A pension fund can qualify through discretion to hire and fire external managers; it need not pick every security itself. Do not confuse managing through external managers with having no investment discretion. [Official Asset Owners Handbook, provisions 21.A.1–3 and 21.A.24](https://www.gipsstandards.org/standards/gips-standards-for-asset-owners/gips-standards-handbook-for-asset-owners/).

> [!NOTE]
> GIPS helps prospective clients, investors, oversight bodies, and firms' internal controls. It does not replace manager due diligence. Relevant GIPS Reports must reach prospective clients and the appropriate pooled-fund prospective investors under the applicable rules.
