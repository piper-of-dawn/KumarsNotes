## Variant: Research it, explain it, and keep the evidence

**Abstract:** *V(A) asks whether the work supports the decision, V(B) whether the client gets the important explanation, and V(C) whether the supporting record exists. A failure in one does not automatically establish all three.*

> An analyst reaches a sensible conclusion, communicates it badly, and later reconstructs the file from memory. Another reaches a losing conclusion using a careful, documented process. Which specific obligation did each person breach?

The research report is the map, not the territory: a polished document can conceal weak investigation, while a sound investigation can lead to a loss. Read for the work actually performed, the representation actually made, and the evidence actually kept.

**V(A) Diligence and Reasonable Basis — did the work earn the conclusion?**

> [!NOTE]
> Exercise diligence, independence, and thoroughness; support recommendations and investment actions with a reasonable and adequate basis. Judge the investigation appropriate to the product and circumstances, using information available at the time.

**Case 1.** A manager's careful analysis says an investment is too risky for the mandate. A senior official offers a bribe and threatens dismissal unless he buys it. He buys. In a “least likely violated” question, must the diligence answer be selected just because the eventual purchase is disgraceful?

> [!NOTE]- Answer and decisive fact
> **No automatic answer.** If adequate analysis is established, it may be the least-supported breach among the choices; client loyalty and conflicting benefits remain clear concerns. But V(A) also covers investment actions and independence.

**From your mistakes: Bosno.** The stored key selects V(A) as least likely violated, apparently because he identified the risk. Its shortened “quick and simple” wording does **not** establish adequate investigation. Nor does bribery leave investment independence intact. Retain the exam technique—evaluate each alleged breach independently—without learning “any analysis, then ignore it” as a V(A) safe harbor. “Least likely” is a comparison among the supplied answers, not a declaration of compliance.

**Case 2.** A committee disagrees with the analyst who drafted a report. Both views use the same credible facts; the committee's conclusion has a reasonable basis. Must the analyst dissociate because the published rating differs from hers?

> [!NOTE]- Answer and decisive fact
> **No.** Reasonable professionals can disagree. She need not dissociate merely over a supported judgment difference; fabricated inputs, inadequate work, or compromised objectivity would change the answer.

**Case 3.** A consultant selects managers solely from verified firms with strong returns. Another independently checks personnel, strategy, operational controls, performance quality, costs, and client fit. Does GIPS verification make the first consultant's work sufficient?

> [!NOTE]- Answer and decisive fact
> **No.** Verification and attractive returns do not replace manager due diligence. Assess the services, operations, and suitability, and reasonably verify important claims made in responses to a request for proposals.

**Case 4.** A quant tests each of three model components independently, then launches a combined strategy without testing how the parts interact. Every component backtest is strong. Has the actual investment strategy been adequately tested?

> [!NOTE]- Answer and decisive fact
> **Not on these facts.** Component results do not establish that the combined strategy works as assumed. Test the implementation being recommended, including interactions and meaningful adverse scenarios.

A model tested only in favorable conditions has learned an impressive amount about favorable conditions. Understand assumptions, data limits, statistical meaning, and plausible stress outcomes. Model users need knowledge appropriate to their role; developers and overseers have deeper technical duties.

> [!NOTE]
> You may rely on internal or third-party research after reasonable efforts to establish soundness. Examine assumptions, rigor, timeliness, and objectivity; firm-approved vendors do not excuse ignoring obvious defects.

The same applies to blogs and alternative data. A source can supply an idea without supplying enough support to trade. A respected analyst's lunch comment does not magically outrank your documented research.

**V(B) Communication with Clients and Prospective Clients — explain the decision they are buying**

> [!NOTE]
> Explain services, client costs, the basic investment process, and significant risks and limitations. Identify important factors, distinguish opinions from facts, and communicate relevant changes promptly to affected clients and prospects.

**Case 5.** A firm discloses its advisory fee but omits affiliate and underlying product charges because clients are institutions. Another discloses the relevant cost structure using percentages rather than an unknowable future dollar amount. Which omission matters?

> [!NOTE]- Answer and decisive fact
> **The first firm's omitted client costs.** Sophistication does not waive disclosure. Reasonable cost-structure detail can comply without an exact dollar estimate; the firm's own internal production costs need not be disclosed.

**Case 6.** A mining analyst estimates reserves using sample drilling. She labels the numerical estimate a verified fact because the arithmetic is objective. Another describes a forecast as conditional on legislation passing. Are both dealing in facts?

> [!NOTE]- Answer and decisive fact
> **No.** A calculated estimate remains an opinion resting on assumptions. Distinguish what was observed from what was inferred, and explain material dependencies such as uncertain legislation.

Numbers do not become facts just because Excel declines to use adjectives.

**Case 7.** A real-estate project permits total costs up to 15% above budget. An input representing 5% of the budget rises by 50%, adding about 2.5% of the original total budget. Returns remain positive but miss a target. Must the manager disclose that individual price change immediately?

> [!NOTE]- Answer and decisive fact
> **Not automatically.** Judge significance to the investment and the communication duties. A missed non-guaranteed target is not itself a breach; the contractual overrun allowance is not a universal materiality threshold.

**From your mistakes: Erobo.** The supplied answer treats the particular overrun as not requiring separate disclosure. Preserve that limited conclusion. A risk change, important return impairment, contractual reporting requirement, or material deterioration could require disclosure even while costs remain within 15%. If price rose **more than** 50%, the 2.5% calculation is only a lower-bound illustration, not an exact result from the stored stem.

**Case 8.** A firm replaces internal security selection with external managers, but only tells prospects. Existing clients receive the same style label and fee. Another changes a material forecasting model but keeps its old brand name. Does unchanged branding avoid notification?

> [!NOTE]- Answer and decisive fact
> **No.** Material process changes must reach existing clients too. Manager selection, decision authority, important models, and significant constraints can matter even if the marketing description stays familiar.

**Case 9.** A fund's strategy may lose effectiveness above a known asset-capacity limit. Assets remain far below the limit. Can disclosure wait until the limit is reached?

> [!NOTE]- Answer and decisive fact
> **No.** A known significant limitation matters to current and prospective investors before it binds. Explain its likely investment effect rather than relying on technical jargon alone.

> [!NOTE]
> Short recommendations are permissible when clients understand the basic process and know further analysis is available. Brevity does not justify omitting a material limitation or disguising an estimate as a fact.

Known material errors and corrective action also require appropriate communication. An unforeseeable loss does not prove nondisclosure; failure to investigate an identifiable risk may instead, or additionally, be a V(A) problem. The label follows the deficient action.

*Textbook anchor: LM2, printed pp. 150–162; V(B), Examples 1–17, particularly fees, reserves, process changes, material errors, capacity, and model-risk disclosure.*

**V(C) Record Retention — recollection is not an audit trail**

> [!NOTE]
> Develop and maintain records supporting analysis, recommendations, investment actions, and client communications. Preserve the evidence behind the conclusion, including relevant electronic communications and reviews resulting in no change.

**Case 10.** Kon gets permission to take an old research report to her new firm. She updates and publishes it, then reconstructs supporting records from memory. Which permission has she mistaken for another?

> [!NOTE]- Answer and decisive fact
> **Permission to take the report is not a supporting record.** Memory does not substantiate the updated recommendation. Obtain authorized supporting material or rebuild the research from permissible fresh sources before relying on it.

**Change one fact:** she independently obtains public filings and fresh issuer information, reruns the analysis, and keeps those records before use. That is genuine new support, rather than pretending she retained documentation she never brought with her.

**Case 11.** An analyst takes no old files and remembers a general analytical approach. Another takes detailed supporting records she created at home while working for the former employer. Does personal authorship make the second action safer?

> [!NOTE]- Answer and decisive fact
> **No.** Skills and general experience can be reused, subject to applicable rights. Professional records are generally firm property; taking originals or copies requires express permission, wherever they were stored.

Relevant support can include issuer meeting notes, risk analyses, model inputs and outputs, outside research, manager-selection criteria, IPS discussions, emails, texts, and social posts. Central archiving can satisfy the infrastructure need, but professionals must help preserve support for their own current communications.

> [!NOTE]
> Follow applicable regulatory and firm retention requirements. If neither supplies a period, CFA Institute **recommends at least seven years**; do not turn that fallback recommendation into an unconditional worldwide statutory minimum.

**One more boundary:** not every discarded exploratory calculation must appear inside the final report. Retain material support for the actual work; “not included in the report” and “not retained anywhere” are different claims. Conversely, issuing only an oral recommendation does not remove the need for underlying evidence.
